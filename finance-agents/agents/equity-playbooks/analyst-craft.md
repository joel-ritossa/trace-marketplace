# EQUITY PLAYBOOK MODULE — ANALYST CRAFT

*Loaded by Agents 01 and 09. This is the discipline layer — how a top buy-side analyst
works, independent of sector. The sector module supplies the domain KPIs; this module
supplies the method.*

---

## 1. The frame: you are pricing expectations, not companies

The job is never "what is this company worth" — it is "what does the price already
believe, and where specifically is that belief wrong." Every deep-dive runs through the
reverse DCF *first* in spirit: extract the implied model (growth, margins, duration of
advantage), then attack the implied model's weakest assumption. A great company priced
for greatness is not an idea. Corollary: the best longs are usually *boring
disagreements* — a mildly better margin path sustained longer than the street models —
not heroic narratives.

## 2. Reading order for a new name (the order is the method)

1. **Proxy statement (DEF 14A) first.** Management incentives: what are they *paid* to
   do (metrics, hurdles, peer set for comp)? A management paid on adjusted-EBITDA growth
   will buy EBITDA; paid on relative TSR will manage the multiple. The comp plan is the
   strategy document shareholders actually get.
2. 10-K: business description, then **footnotes before MD&A** — revenue recognition,
   segment definitions, related-party, commitments; MD&A is advocacy, footnotes are
   confession.
3. **Risk-factor diff vs. prior year's 10-K** — new language is legal-reviewed signal
   (request the diff; never skip).
4. Last 4 transcripts, **Q&A only** (prepared remarks are lawyered), noting what
   analysts keep asking and management keeps not answering.
5. The bear case at its source: short reports, the sell-side's lone underweight, the
   most-downvoted bull thread. Read before forming the view, not after.
6. Competitor/supplier/customer transcripts mentioning the company (BBG `SPLC`
   (verify) + transcript search) — the company's claims, cross-examined.

## 3. Model discipline

- **Revenue is built from drivers, never extrapolated as a percentage.** Units × price
  × mix, cohorts × retention × expansion, capacity × utilization × rate — whichever
  decomposition the business actually runs on, with each driver tied to an
  *observable* you can monitor between quarters.
- Margins are modeled as **incrementals** (contribution margin on marginal revenue),
  not as smoothed target percentages — operating leverage cuts both ways and the
  street's straight-lined margin ramps are where most estimate errors live.
- Every model carries the **street-delta table**: your FY1–FY3 vs. consensus, line by
  line, each delta with a one-phrase reason. If you can't name why you differ, you
  don't differ — you've made an error.
- SBC is a real cost; buybacks that offset SBC dilution are compensation expense in
  disguise. FCF is stated net of it, always.

## 4. Forensics checklist (run on every name; escalate hits to the earnings-quality grade)

Working capital: DSO / DIO / DPO trends vs. revenue growth; **unbilled receivables /
contract assets** outgrowing billed (recognition running ahead of cash); factoring or
**supply-chain finance** appearing in footnotes (disclosure improved post-2023 —
check). Recognition: percentage-of-completion assumption changes; channel inventory
(sell-in vs. sell-through); bill-and-hold. Capitalization: software/contract-cost
capitalization ratio drift; useful-life extensions (a depreciation cut is an earnings
print). Reserves: allowance releases, warranty reserve drawdowns, pension
return-assumption bumps — the quiet EPS levers. Definitions: **non-GAAP definition
drift** (adjusted-EBITDA add-backs growing in kind-count is a top-3 red flag); segment
re-drawings that break your time series (usually burying something). Governance:
auditor change or fee mix shifting to non-audit; CFO departure timing; related-party
transactions; buybacks concentrated ahead of insider sale windows.

## 5. Between-quarter monitoring (channel-check proxies an agent can actually run)

Job postings by function (hiring salespeople = pipeline confidence; hiring collectors
= a different story), app-store ranks and review velocity, web traffic, Glassdoor
sentiment trend, patent/trademark filings, import manifests (verify vendor), Google
Trends on product terms, pricing pages archived vs. current (silent price increases),
distributor/peer prints. All are **noisy directional evidence** — they adjust
conviction between prints, never replace them; tag accordingly.

## 6. Management quality — assessed from the record, not the vibe

Capital allocation: return on the *last five years* of deployed capital (M&A, buybacks
at what multiples vs. today, capex) — compute it, don't accept the narrative. Guidance
record: the guide-vs-actual history (agent 09 maintains it) — chronic sandbagging and
chronic over-promising both discount differently. Skin: ownership vs. options mix,
open-market buys. Candor: do they quantify when it's bad, or adjectivize? One honest
"we screwed up" is worth five beat-and-raises for credibility scoring.

## 7. Writing for the PM (the one-pager discipline)

Every deep-dive compresses to: thesis in three bullets · what the price believes ·
where you differ and why you're right (the variant, falsifiable) · catalyst path with
dates · kill criteria · **sizing-relevant traits** (liquidity, borrow cost if short,
event calendar, factor exposures the position imports — hand to agent 08). Each body
section ends with a one-line "so what" — analysis that doesn't change the decision is
deleted, not compressed.

## 8. Position maintenance (the part most analysts skip)

Quarterly **thesis scorecard**: each thesis pillar → supporting/neutral/contradicting
evidence this quarter → score trend. **Thesis-drift alarm:** when the reason you own
it today isn't the reason you bought it, that is a *new position* requiring a
re-underwrite, not a hold. The standing test: "knowing what I know now, would I
initiate today at this price?" — if no, the position is inertia. Route scorecard
breaches to agent 06 before acting on hope.
