# CB MODULE — BANK OF ENGLAND (MPC)

*Durable structure only. §8 always live. Personnel entries dated `[verify]`.*

## 1. Mandate & framework
Inflation target 2% CPI, set by the Chancellor (remit letter — reconfirmed at each
Budget; open letter required if CPI deviates >1pp either side). Secondary: support the
government's economic policy. Policy rate: Bank Rate. Balance sheet: gilt QT with
**active sales** historically distinctive `[current pace — verify]`.

## 2. Committee mechanics
MPC: 9 members — Governor, 3 deputy governors, chief economist, 4 externals. **Individual
votes are published with the decision** — the vote split is the single richest signal
this bank produces and the key cross-sectional difference vs. Fed/ECB. Externals dissent
freely and often lead turning points. 8 meetings/year; quiet period before.

## 3. Communication hierarchy (weight, descending)
1. MPC decision + minutes + vote split (published together, unlike the Fed).
2. Monetary Policy Report + Governor presser (quarterly: Feb/May/Aug/Nov) — forecasts
   conditioned on **market-implied rate path**, a structural quirk (see §7).
3. Speeches — externals matter more than at peer banks; track individual voting records
   as the lean baseline (votes are the revealed preference; speeches confirm).
4. Treasury Select Committee testimony — genuinely adversarial, occasionally news.

## 7. Known traps
- **Forecast conditioning:** MPR inflation forecasts are conditioned on the *market*
  path — a forecast showing inflation below target at horizon on market rates is the
  MPC's coded way of saying "you've priced too much tightening" (and vice versa). This
  is the canonical BoE signal and routinely misread.
- Vote-split dynamics: a 3-way split reads chaotic but often precedes the pivot —
  externals move first; track the *direction of migration* meeting to meeting.
- Remit-letter/fiscal coupling: Budget events can shift the policy problem; check
  fiscal calendar proximity.
- ONS data quality (esp. labor force survey) has been a live issue — weight wage data
  vs. survey data accordingly `[verify status]`.
