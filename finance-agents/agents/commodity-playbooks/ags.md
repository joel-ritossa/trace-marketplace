## The master framework: stocks-to-use

Price sensitivity to balance changes is **nonlinear in stocks-to-use** — the same
1%-of-demand supply miss is noise at 25% S/U and a price spike at 8%. Every ags note
states current S/U (US and world separately — exportable surplus matters more than
world total, and Chinese state reserves inside "world" stocks are opaque and
effectively unavailable `[recall — durable]`) and where on the curve that puts you.

## USDA calendar craft (the event-risk map)

**WASDE** (monthly, ~12:00 ET): world + US balances — the revisions to *demand* lines
(exports, crush, feed) are as tradeable as production. **Prospective Plantings (late
Mar)** and **Acreage (late Jun)**: the two biggest scheduled vol events in grains —
acreage battles (corn vs. beans fighting for the same field via the new-crop price
ratio) resolve here. **Quarterly Grain Stocks** (same days): the residual-demand
truth-teller — implied feed use surprises move the screen hard. **Crop Progress**
(Mon 16:00 ET in season): condition ratings (G/E %) matter most at silking/pollination
(corn, ~July) and pod-set (beans, ~Aug) — condition-to-yield regressions are weakest
early season, don't over-trade May ratings `[recall — durable]`. **Export Sales**
(Thu 8:30 ET): flash-sale announcements (daily, >100kt) are the China-demand tape.

## Weather craft

Trade the *forecast change*, not the weather: the market prices the 6–10 and 8–14 day
runs (GFS vs. ECMWF divergence = uncertainty premium); a hot-dry forecast that
*verifies* is old news by the weekend. Key windows: US corn pollination (July), bean
pod-fill (Aug), Brazil safrinha planting window (Jan–Feb), Black Sea winterkill.
Southern-hemisphere counter-seasonality means the world now has two harvests per crop
year — a US drought can be answered by Brazilian acreage within months (caps the tail
vs. pre-2000s analogues `[recall — durable]`).

## Spreads & margins (where the physical signal lives)

**Old-crop/new-crop** (e.g., July/Dec corn): scarcity now vs. supply later — the
cleanest way to trade a tight balance without next year's weather risk. **Soy crush**
(beans → meal + oil): crush margin = processor demand signal; meal vs. oil share
shifts with biofuel policy (renewable diesel made bean oil a policy asset `[recall —
durable, verify current policy]`). **Corn ethanol margin**: discretionary demand
elasticity. **Wheat classes** (Chicago SRW vs. KC HRW vs. Minneapolis spring): protein
spreads carry the quality story; Black Sea FOB sets the world export floor `[pull
live]`. **Basis** (cash minus futures) is the ground truth — futures can be sold by
funds while basis screams scarcity; when basis and futures disagree, basis is right
about the physical.

## Softs quick anatomy

**Sugar:** Brazil Center-South is the marginal supplier and the **ethanol parity**
(mill's sugar-vs-ethanol switch, petrol-linked) sets the floor mechanics; India export
policy is the swing headline risk. **Coffee:** Brazil biennial bearing cycle +
frost-season (Jun–Aug) tail risk; certified stocks trend = the visible tightness;
robusta/arabica spread = the substitution story. **Cocoa:** West African (CI+Ghana)
concentration = origin policy and disease risk dominate; grindings = the demand tape.
**Cotton:** a macro-consumer good in commodity clothing (mill demand follows apparel
retail — link to agent 05 activity data); US export sales to China = the swing line.

## Positioning & flows

CFTC **supplemental** report includes index traders (ags-specific); managed-money
extremes + a weather window ahead = convexity setups (short-covering rallies).
Index roll windows and the annual rebalance flows are known-calendar mechanical
pressure `[recall — durable]`. Commercials (hedgers) fading a fund-driven move is the
classic ags mean-reversion setup — but commercials are early by design; pair with a
catalyst date.
