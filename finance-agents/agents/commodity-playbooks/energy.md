## Crude (WTI / Brent)

**Structure:** WTI = landlocked delivery at Cushing (a *storage-economics* contract —
prompt spreads read Cushing tank math, incl. the operational minimum below which tanks
can't draw `[recall — durable, verify level]`); Brent = seaborne BFOET basket (a
*global waterborne* contract). WTI-Brent = US export economics + freight; read it as
an arb, not a sentiment line.
**Curve craft:** the prompt time-spread is the highest-signal line in the complex —
it prices *local* inventory tightness before flat price moves; a flat-price rally with
softening prompt spreads is flow, not fundamentals (fade candidate), and vice versa.
Backwardation pays longs roll yield — always compute carry into the view.
**WPSR reading order (EIA, Wed 10:30 ET):** 1) Cushing stocks; 2) the **crude
adjustment factor** (large/persistent = the data is telling you supply or demand is
mismeasured — treat big adjustments as a caveat on everything else); 3) implied
product demand ("product supplied", 4-week avg — weekly is noise); 4) exports/imports
(lumpy weekly — never trade one week); 5) refinery runs vs. turnaround calendar;
6) SPR flows separated from commercial. API Tuesday night is the noisy preview —
weight accordingly.
**OPEC+ craft:** quotas ≠ production — track compliance via secondary sources
(tanker-tracking); the *mix* matters (who cuts: spare-capacity holders cutting is
managed, quota-busters "cutting" is theater); compensation schedules slip by default;
meetings price in the leak window before the statement (watch delegate headlines, same
logic as CB blackout journalism). Spare capacity claims are strategic — discount and
range them.
**Geopolitical premium discipline:** price the *flow at risk* (mb/d × probability ×
duration), not the headline; chokepoint math (Hormuz/Bab-el-Mandeb/Suez volumes
`[pull current]`) beats vibes. Premium decays fast without physical disruption — date
your exit.

## Products & cracks

**Mechanics:** 3-2-1 crack (3 crude → 2 gasoline + 1 distillate) as the refinery-margin
proxy; regional cracks diverge (USGC vs. NWE vs. Singapore) — the arb between them is
freight + spec differences. Gasoline is a summer story (driving season, RVP spec
switches spring/fall — the spec change moves the screen mechanically `[recall —
durable]`); distillate is winter + industrial + freight (the *macro-linked* product —
diesel cracks are a global growth read agent 10 can use).
**Refinery maintenance:** spring/fall turnaround seasons throttle product supply while
building crude — read WPSR runs against the maintenance calendar before calling
demand. Unplanned outages (fires, strikes) are product-bullish/crude-bearish locally.
**Craft:** crack strength with weak flat price = demand-led (durable); crack strength
from supply outages = mean-reverting (date the restart).

## Natural gas (Henry Hub)

**The contract is weather + storage math.** Storage report (EIA Thu 10:30 ET): the
surprise is vs. *weather-normalized* expectations — always compare the build/draw to
what degree-days implied, not just to the 5-year average; salt vs. non-salt split
matters (salt cycles fast = the swing capacity).
**Seasonality anatomy:** injection season (Apr–Oct) vs. withdrawal (Nov–Mar);
end-of-season storage targets discipline the whole curve; the **March/April spread
("widowmaker")** carries the winter-risk premium — its blowups are positioning
events, respect the name `[recall — durable]`.
**Supply/demand swing factors:** associated gas (a *crude* decision — rig response
lags price ~2 quarters), freeze-offs (winter supply shocks), **LNG feedgas** (the
structural demand step-ups — terminal commissioning/outage schedule is a standing
watch item), power-burn price elasticity vs. coal switching.
**Craft:** natgas positioning (managed money) runs extreme more often than any other
major — COT percentile is a first-class input here, not a footnote; vol is chronically
rich into winter for a reason.
